# Cloud, puddle and traversal checks — 1.5.1-preview.3

The reflection sky now owns separate history and a minimal unused radiance
cubemap. The visible reflection cache remains 256×144 at 4K and High cloud ray
samples are unchanged. Both final reflected-sky captures match the corresponding
preview.2 images byte for byte.

A larger CPU cost came from rebinding the same three cloud noise textures every
frame. Godot's global sampler setter queues texture updates on all materials
using that sampler, including unchanged textures. Binding immutable noise only
when created removes that repeated work. This behavior was verified in
`MaterialStorage::global_shader_parameter_set` in the local Godot engine source.

## Final Mines camera sweep

Linux Ubuntu 24.04, Forward+ Vulkan, RTX 3090 Ti, 3840×2160, Very High and HD Lands
1.4.0. Both arms use the same shipping engine, private quicksave, options, cloud
clock and camera path. The complete scene renders in a 4K viewport with a blank
small root, excluding large desktop presentation transfers. Each case measures
960 frames after eight seconds settling. The camera traverses 45 metres and
returns. Visual-effect streaming continues; AI and game simulation are stopped.
This measures traversal rendering, not end-to-end gameplay FPS.

Clouds are Volume: High, with shadows and reflections enabled. Times are
milliseconds; lower is better.

The sampled Mines endpoints have no visible reflective water, so this sweep
primarily establishes the shadow-related CPU improvement. The shadow-field GPU

The raw record retains the full frame distributions and long-frame samples.
These results do not establish that every traversal hitch has been eliminated.

## Shoreline and reflection visibility

The baseline matrix includes clouds off, sky only, shadows, reflections and both
consumers. The final pack repeats the two camera poses with both consumers on,
using 240 frames after four seconds settling and forty subsequent GPU samples.

| overhead | 0.858 | 0.348 | 12.368 | 11.652 |

The final two cache images are pixel-identical to the baseline. Whole-scene gains
are smaller than reflection-pass gains because that pass is only part of a frame.

Water previously omitted the two-percent normal-incidence Fresnel response.
Restoring it keeps reflections present from steep isometric angles, for scenery
as well as sky and clouds. Mirror's scenery multiplier remains in place; swamp
tint and water colour limits are retained. Overhead reflections remain subtler
than grazing-angle reflections. The rendered contract checks the normal-incidence
floor and scenery occlusion for the cloud sampling path.

## Puddles and particle synchronization

Puddle depressions used the terrain-detail noise texture, but the texture was
only bound when terrain detail was enabled. It now binds whenever puddles need
it. Standing water now occupies only small, sparse depressions, with a receding
wet margin. A two-percent normal-incidence Fresnel response preserves the ground
colour from above; the sky sheen increases at shallow angles. Terrain and scenery
contact blending use the same mask and colour response. Rendered checks cover
original and detailed HD terrain from isometric and shallow angles, sparse coverage
at full wetness, persistence after rain stops, progressive drying, roof exclusion,
dry-ground equality and exact restoration when toggled off.
These are the existing glossy wet-surface puddles, not separate planar mirrors.

Particle updates previously posted a task for every pool thread and joined every
task, even with fewer emitters. Groups now use at most four tasks and never more
than the emitter count. A seeded comparison verifies exact particle records,
future RNG state, draw bytes, culling bounds and lifetimes against serial and
full-pool scheduling for 3, 8 and 32 emitters. The eight-emitter p95 round cost was
0.422 ms with full-pool scheduling and 0.316 ms with four tasks. This is a CPU
microbenchmark, not a whole-frame saving. Dense 32-emitter costs were similar.
The previous preview's grass-streaming, footprint preparation and snow-track
material fixes are retained.

## Regression coverage and limits

A proposed shared/indexed water-plane query was withdrawn; the original selection
path is retained. A diagnostic then traced intermittent camera-test gaps to the
fixture changing its camera in a deferred post-draw callback after the reflection
process had already run. The fixture now moves at process start and verifies
that it captures the first submitted frame. The shipping pack passes unchanged
pixel thresholds and still detects the interpolation-lag negative control. Cloud ownership/release, shadow projection, paused camera
motion, lightning, option toggles, reflection occlusion and Compatibility water
are covered separately. Translation coverage is 1,174 entries with no errors;
no player-visible strings were added.

The [machine-readable record](clouds-2026-10-15.json) includes source and pack
hashes, configurations, per-case distributions and fixture hashes. Tests use
original game data provided locally; private inputs are not published. Older
retained regression results are identified by their pack hashes and have the
same relevant cloud/water code; final camera and performance checks use the
shipping pack.

No Windows/D3D12 or RTX 4090 laptop measurement is available. Linux results are
not a promised FPS gain on that platform. Forced-render-loop diagnostics and
experimental live-simulation viewports with stale GPU timings are excluded from
performance claims. Benchmark images are read only outside timed loops.
